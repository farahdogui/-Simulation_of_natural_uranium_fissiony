Project Overview

This scientific computing project applies probabilistic Monte Carlo methods to study the criticality of Natural Uranium (0.7% U-235). Starting from a simplified mathematical model, the project evolves into a physically accurate engine that integrates continuous energy dependence, nuclear cross-sections based on ENDF data, and the Watt Spectrum for fission neutron energy distribution.

The simulation correctly validates that natural uranium in an infinite medium is subcritical (k_eff < 1) due to the resonance capture phenomenon in U-238, demonstrating the "resonance trap" effect that prevents sustained chain reactions.

Part 1: Physics Engine

Nuclear Data Foundation

The physics engine is built on nuclear cross-section data based on ENDF/B-VIII evaluations for both U-235 and U-238 isotopes. Cross-sections for fission, radiative capture, elastic scattering, and inelastic scattering are stored on logarithmic energy grids spanning from 1e-5 eV (thermal) to 20 MeV (fast).

Energy-Dependent Cross-Sections

  • U-235 thermal fission: ~584 barns at 0.0253 eV
  • U-238 fission threshold: negligible below 1 MeV
  • Resonance region: U-238 capture peaks around 10 eV
  • Inelastic scattering: threshold at ~40-45 keV for both isotopes

Watt Spectrum Implementation

Fission neutron energies are sampled from the Watt Spectrum using rejection sampling:

f(E) = C e^(-aE) sinh(sqrt(bE))
with a = 0.988, b = 2.249 (MeV^-1)

Log-Log Interpolation

Cross-sections between grid points are computed using linear interpolation in log-log space, accurately capturing the steep 1/v behavior in the thermal region and resonance structures.

Competitive Processes

Each neutron interaction competes between four outcomes: fission, radiative capture, inelastic scattering, and elastic scattering - with probabilities dynamically calculated from the neutron's current energy.

Part 2: Monte Carlo Simulation

Neutron Life Cycle

The simulation follows a generation-based random walk approach, tracking each neutron through its complete life cycle:

  • Initialization: 1000 thermal neutrons (0.025 eV) injected into natural uranium
  • Isotope Selection: Macroscopic probability weighting (0.7% U-235 vs 99.3% U-238)
  • Interaction Sampling: Fission, capture, or scattering chosen by cross-section ratios
  • Fission Events: 2-3 new neutrons born with Watt Spectrum energies (MeV range)
  • Moderation: Elastic and inelastic collisions progressively slow down neutrons

Generation Tracking

Neutron population is recorded at each generation, allowing visualization of population dynamics and detection of convergence, decay, or exponential growth.

Population Bounds

Safety limits prevent runaway computation: simulation stops if population reaches zero (reaction dies) or exceeds 10,000 neutrons (reaction explodes).

Part 3: Results & Validation

Population Dynamics

The simulation output reveals the characteristic behavior of natural uranium under neutron bombardment:

  • Initial spike: Thermal neutrons encounter the high fission cross-section of U-235
  • Fast birth: Fission neutrons are born in the MeV range
  • Resonance trap: Slowing-down neutrons are captured by U-238 resonances
  • Subcritical decay: Population stabilizes below criticality

Physical Validation

The calculated k_eff ~0.983 confirms the well-established physics that natural uranium cannot sustain a chain reaction without enrichment or a moderator. This validates the simulation engine against known nuclear physics.

Resonance Trap Effect

Unlike simplified models that predict infinite growth, the physics-aware model correctly captures the U-238 resonance capture phenomenon that dominates the intermediate energy range (1-1000 eV).

Key Insights & Physical Implications

Natural Uranium Limitations

The simulation demonstrates why natural uranium alone cannot sustain a chain reaction. The 99.3% U-238 content acts as a neutron poison through resonance capture, requiring enrichment or moderation for reactor applications.

Energy Spectrum Evolution

Neutrons born fast (MeV) rapidly lose energy through scattering. This slowing-down process is where U-238 captures neutrons most effectively, creating the resonance trap that limits criticality.

Reactor Design Insights

The results explain fundamental reactor design choices: enrichment increases fissile content, while moderators like water or graphite rapidly thermalize neutrons to avoid the resonance region entirely.

Methodological Strengths

This simulation demonstrates the power of Monte Carlo methods for nuclear physics. The combination of ENDF-based cross-section data, the Watt Spectrum, and generation-based random walks provides a physically accurate representation of neutron transport. The correct prediction of subcriticality validates both the mathematical model and the computational implementation.

Future Research Directions

Potential extensions include adding moderating materials (water, graphite), simulating enriched uranium compositions, implementing spatial geometry with leakage effects, and comparing results against established reactor physics codes. These enhancements would enable the study of full reactor core configurations and criticality benchmarks.
